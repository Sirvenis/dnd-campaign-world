## 1. World Concept

### The Pitch

A world where the gods are dead — slain by each other in a war that shattered the heavens three centuries ago. Their divine blood soaked into the earth, saturating it with raw power that mortals now mine, refine, and weaponise. Civilisation clings to the ruins of a golden age it can no longer fully understand, powered by the corpse-light of dead divinities. But the blood is running out, and the things it does to people who touch it too long are getting worse.

### The Tone

Dark fantasy with warmth at the centre. The world is broken, but people are not. Communities hold together through stubbornness, humour, and mutual care even as the institutions around them crumble. The horror is real — places where the divine blood pools create monsters, mutations, and madness — but the player characters are fighting to protect what's good, not to save a world that was already lost.

Think: the weight of DragonLance's Cataclysm (gods are gone, the old world is over) combined with the faction-driven sandbox of Drakkenheim (competing groups with different visions for the future) and the character-focused storytelling of Exandria (relationships drive the plot).

### The Central Tension

The divine blood — called **residuum** — is the foundation of everything. It powers the remaining magic, fuels the old machines, lights the cities, and heals the sick. But:

1. **It's finite.** The gods are not coming back. What blood soaked into the earth three centuries ago is all there will ever be. The great reserves are dwindling.
2. **It's dangerous.** Prolonged exposure causes physical and mental corruption — the **Ashening** — transforming people into something not quite human.
3. **It's contested.** Every faction has a different idea about what to do with the remaining supply: hoard it, share it, destroy it, use it to ascend to godhood, or find a way to revive the dead gods.

This creates an inherently unstable situation that generates adventure hooks naturally: every decision about residuum is a decision about the future of the world, and every faction has a stake.

### What Makes This World Different from DragonLance

| Element | DragonLance (Krynn) | Aethermere |
|---------|---------------------|------------|
| Gods | Withdrew but still exist | Dead. Genuinely dead. Their bodies are in the earth. |
| Cataclysm | A mountain fell on Istar | The gods went to war with each other and all died |
| Magic source | Gods grant power to clerics; moons power wizardry | Divine blood in the ground (residuum). No gods to grant anything. |
| Time since catastrophe | ~300 years (Age of Despair) | ~300 years (the Godswar ended 300 years ago) |
| Divine intervention | Gods return and actively meddle | Gods are dead. They cannot return. The question is what replaces them. |
| Central question | Can good defeat evil? | What do mortals do when the gods are gone and the power they left behind is running out? |
| Tone | Epic high fantasy, clear good vs evil | Dark fantasy with moral complexity. No faction is wholly right or wrong. |

## 2. Cosmology

### The Godswar and the Shattering

Three centuries ago, the gods went to war. Not over mortal souls or alignment philosophy — over **the Throne of Heaven**, the seat of ultimate authority over the world's metaphysical laws. The details are lost (every culture tells the story differently), but the outcome is undeniable: every god died. They destroyed each other so thoroughly that not even their names survived intact.

When the gods died, their divine essence — raw creative power, the stuff that holds reality together — bled into the earth. This was the **Shattering**: the sky cracked, continents shifted, seas boiled, and the divine blood soaked into the ground, the rivers, the mountains, and the living things that survived.

The world's metaphysical infrastructure broke. The afterlife severed — souls of the dead now have nowhere to go, lingering as ghosts or dissolving into ambient energy. Divine magic ceased (no gods to grant it). Arcane magic continued but changed — it now draws on residuum in the earth rather than the Weave the gods maintained.

### The Afterlife Problem

With the gods dead, the Outer Planes are unmoored. The afterlife doesn't work properly:
- Souls don't pass on. They linger as spirits, fade into the ambient energy, or — rarely — become something else.
- Necromancy is more common and more dangerous. Without the natural cycle of death-and-judgement, the dead are restless.
- Some cultures have developed rituals to help souls "dissolve" peacefully. Others bind them as ancestors. Others fear and destroy them.
- The Outer Planes still exist but are decaying, unguided. Planar travel is possible but dangerous — the planes are unstable, shifting, and occupied by the remnants of celestial and fiendish armies that have no commanders.

### The Residuum

Divine blood in the earth. It's the world's most valuable resource.

- **Appearance**: A luminescent liquid that seeps from rock, pools in caves, and runs in veins through the earth like gold ore. It glows with a pale, shifting light — white-gold, blue-violet, or sickly green depending on concentration and purity.
- **Uses**: Powers magic (spellcasters tap into it), fuels ancient machines, can heal wounds, can extend life, can create magical items, and — theoretically — could be used to do what the gods did: reshape reality itself.
- **Danger**: Prolonged exposure causes the **Ashening**. Skin develops a faint ash-grey pallor. Eyes become luminescent. Eventually, the person loses their sense of self, becoming a **Hollow** — a powerful but mindless being of raw divine energy. This process takes years of heavy exposure; casual contact with diluted residuum is safe.
- **Finitude**: The gods aren't making more. The great underground reservoirs are being drained. Smaller surface deposits are nearly exhausted. Within a generation or two, the supply will become critical.
- **Geography**: Residuum is not evenly distributed. Some regions sit on rich deposits (and are correspondingly wealthy, dangerous, and contested). Others are bone-dry and must import it at great expense.

### Cosmological Model

Aethermere uses a **simplified cosmology** (one of the options described in the 2024 DMG):

- **The Material Plane**: The world of Aethermere itself.
- **The Echoes**: The Feywild (still intact, though strained) and the Shadowfell (expanding — the dead have nowhere to go, and the Shadowfell absorbs them).
- **The Transitive Planes**: The Ethereal Plane (thinning) and the Astral Plane (choked with the petrified corpses of dead gods — literal mountains of divine stone drifting in the silver void).
- **The Elemental Planes**: Intact but wild — without the gods to regulate the boundaries, elemental incursions are more common.
- **The Outer Planes**: Decaying. The Upper Planes are dimming. The Lower Planes are growing hungrier. The armies of celestials and fiends that served the gods are leaderless, some forming their own petty kingdoms, others still fighting a war that no longer has a purpose.
- **The Far Realm**: Pressing at the edges of reality. With the gods' protective influence gone, things from beyond are taking an interest.

This gives us:
- Planar travel adventures (visiting decaying Outer Planes, encountering leaderless celestial/fiend armies)
- Elemental incursions as a recurring threat
- The Astral Plane as a creepy location full of dead god corpses (potential high-level adventure site)
- The Shadowfell expansion as a growing problem tied to the afterlife crisis
- Far Realm incursions as late-campaign cosmic horror

## 3. The World's History — The Three Ages

### Age of Gods (~300+ years ago and earlier)

The gods ruled the world directly. They walked among mortals, granted magic to their followers, maintained the cosmic machinery (the afterlife, the planar boundaries, the elemental balance), and — crucially — kept each other in check through a system of alliances, rivalries, and the Balance.

This was a golden age by most accounts, though not a peaceful one. The gods were not a united pantheon — they were a fractious family of immensely powerful beings with different values, different followers, and different visions for the world. The Balance was the treaty that kept them from destroying each other, and by extension, the world.

Key features of this age:
- Divine magic was abundant — clerics could channel their god's power directly
- The afterlife functioned — souls went to their destined Outer Plane
- Planar boundaries were stable — few elemental incursions, no Far Realm leaks
- Civilization was advanced — the gods taught mortals to build, farm, and create
- The gods maintained the Weave — the metaphysical infrastructure that made arcane magic safe and reliable

**The Godswar**: No one knows exactly what started it. The stories contradict each other. What's known:
- The Balance broke. Some gods sought to rule alone rather than share power.
- The war was total. Every god chose a side (or was destroyed for not choosing).
- The gods killed each other. Not one survived. Their deaths were mutually assured destruction.
- The Shattering followed: the sky cracked, the earth shook, divine blood soaked into the ground, the Weave collapsed, the afterlife broke, and the planar boundaries weakened.
- The war lasted years. Mortals were caught in the crossfire. Entire cities were destroyed. Populations were decimated.

### Age of Ash (~300 years ago to ~100 years ago)

The aftermath. Two centuries of darkness, famine, and chaos.

- **The Long Dark**: For decades after the Godswar, the sky was choked with divine ash. Crops failed. The surviving mortals huddled in shelters, praying to gods who could not answer.
- **The Broken Magic**: Arcane magic went haywire. Without the Weave, spells were unpredictable, dangerous, and sometimes lethal. Many wizards died trying to cast spells the old way. It took generations for mortals to discover that the residuum in the earth could substitute for the Weave — that magic could be drawn from the gods' blood.
- **The Restless Dead**: With the afterlife broken, ghosts were everywhere. The first century was haunted — literally. Communities developed rituals to lay spirits to rest, but none were fully effective.
- **The Wars of Faith**: Former clerics and paladins, bereft of their gods' power, went mad or turned violent. Some led crusades to "avenge" their dead gods. Others became warlords. The holy orders fractured.
- **Rediscovery**: Slowly, over generations, survivors learned to work with what remained. They found the residuum. They learned to refine it. They rebuilt.

### Age of Residuum (~100 years ago to present)

The current age. Civilization has rebuilt, but it's built on a foundation of dead gods.

- **The City-States**: Without the gods to unify the world, civilization fragmented into city-states and small kingdoms, each controlling what residuum deposits they can.
- **The Residuum Economy**: Residuum is the currency of power. It's traded, hoarded, fought over, and refined. The great refineries are the most important industrial sites in the world.
- **The Factions**: Different groups have formed around different answers to the central question: what do we do with the remaining residuum? This is the engine of political conflict.
- **The Ashening**: As residuum use has increased, so have cases of the Ashening. Hollows — people who've been corrupted by overexposure — are a growing problem. Some are dangerous monsters. Others are tragic figures, still recognisably human, slowly losing themselves.
- **The New Magic**: Arcane magic works, but it's different. Wizards tap into residuum rather than the Weave. Sorcerers are sometimes born with residuum in their blood — a legacy of the Shattering. Warlocks make pacts with the remnants of celestial/fiendish armies on the Outer Planes. Clerics and druids draw on ambient divine energy in the earth — it's weaker than what the gods granted, and it's slowly fading.
- **The Frontier**: Beyond the safe regions are the Shatterlands — areas where the Godswar's destruction was most intense. These are rich in residuum but dangerous: unstable terrain, elemental anomalies, monsters, Hollows, and ruins of the old world. Adventurers venture into them for wealth, knowledge, and residuum.

## 4. Geography — The Known World

### Design Principle: Start Small

This document defines the **starting region** in detail and the wider world in broad strokes. The wider world expands as the campaign grows.

### The Starting Region: The Ember Coast

A coastal region on the eastern edge of the known world, centered on the city-state of **Hallow's Reach**. The Ember Coast was the site of a major battle during the Godswar — the gods' blood soaked deep into the earth here, making it one of the richest residuum regions in the world.

**Key Features:**
- **Hallow's Reach**: The largest city in the Ember Coast. Population ~40,000. Built on the ruins of a holy city from the Age of Gods. Controls the richest residuum mine in the region — the **Godvein**, a vast underground deposit discovered 80 years ago. The city is wealthy, tense, and crowded. Refineries line the harbour. The ruling family — the **Hallow family** — controls the Godvein and the refinery operations.
- **The Godvein**: The richest known residuum deposit in the Ember Coast. A vast underground network of tunnels and caverns where divine blood pools in luminous lakes. It's been mined for 80 years and is showing signs of depletion. It's also dangerous — the deeper you go, the more concentrated the residuum, and the faster the Ashening takes hold.
- **The Shatterlands (West)**: West of Hallow's Reach, the land is scarred. The Godswar's final battle happened here. The terrain is unstable — gravity fluctuates, elemental anomalies appear and disappear, and ruins of Age of Gods cities lie broken and half-buried. Rich in residuum but extremely dangerous. Adventurers venture here for salvage and mining.
- **Emberwood Village**: A small settlement 5 miles from the Shatterlands' edge. A frontier town where adventurers stock up before heading into the Shatterlands. Has a rough, boom-town feel. Governed informally by a council of merchants and retired adventurers.
- **The Drowned Temple**: An Age of Gods temple complex, half-submerged in a residuum lake in the Shatterlands. Dedicated to a god whose name has been lost. Still contains powerful artifacts and knowledge from the old world — but also guardians, traps, and concentrated residuum that accelerates the Ashening.
- **The Pale Reach (North)**: A mountainous region where the divine ash from the Godswar never fully settled. The mountains are grey-white with divine ash. Sparsely populated. Home to monastic orders who have developed techniques to slow the Ashening and to help souls "dissolve" peacefully. Also home to exiles and hermits who prefer isolation.
- **The Saltmarch (South)**: Coastal wetlands and fishing villages. Poor in residuum. The people here live simply and have a strong oral tradition of the old world. They are suspicious of "blood magic" (their term for residuum-based magic) but are not hostile. They practice a form of ancestor veneration — binding the souls of their dead as protective spirits, since the afterlife doesn't work.

### The Wider World (Broad Strokes)

- **The Iron Concord**: A federation of city-states to the west, united by a military pact. They control large residuum reserves and have the most organised army in the known world. Tend toward authoritarian order — they see controlled residuum distribution as the path to stability.
- **The Silver Isles**: An island nation to the south, known for their merchant fleet and their mastery of residuum refining. They don't have many natural deposits — they buy raw residuum and refine it into more potent forms. Wealthy, cosmopolitan, and politically neutral (they sell to everyone).
- **The Pale Theocracy**: A theocratic state to the north, built on the worship of the **dead gods**. They believe the gods will return if enough faith is demonstrated. They hoard residuum for use in massive resurrection rituals (which have never worked). Governed by former clerics who have developed a form of "echo divine magic" — weak, unreliable, but genuine. Desperate and growing more extreme.
- **The Wilds**: Uncharted regions where civilization hasn't rebuilt. Full of ruins, monsters, and unexplored residuum deposits. Beyond the Wilds, the world is simply unknown — no one has explored far enough to know what's there.

## 5. The Factions

Using the research from our Faction Design document, these factions are built on the "axis faction + absolutist + status quo seeker" triangle model, with interlocking tensions.

### The Five Major Factions of the Ember Coast

**1. The Hallow Compact** (Status Quo Seeker)
- **Who**: The ruling family of Hallow's Reach and their allies — the refinery owners, the city guard, the merchant guilds who profit from the residuum trade.
- **Goal**: Maintain control of the Godvein and the current residuum economy. Keep Hallow's Reach stable and profitable. Resist any change that would disrupt their power.
- **Resources**: The Godvein (richest deposit), the refineries, the city guard, trade wealth, political legitimacy.
- **Limitation**: They're dependent on adventurers to explore the Shatterlands and bring back salvage/residuum, but they don't fully control them. Their authority is economic, not military — they couldn't withstand a full-scale assault.
- **NPC leader**: **Lady Seren Hallow** — the matriarch, in her sixties, sharp and pragmatic. She understands the residuum is running out but doesn't know what to do about it. She fears change more than she fears depletion.

**2. The Ashborn** (Absolutist / Reform)
- **Who**: People who have undergone partial Ashening and embraced it rather than fighting it. They see the divine blood as a gift, not a curse. They believe mortals should absorb the residuum and become something greater — the next gods. Led by **The Ashen Choir**, a council of powerful Hollows who have retained their minds (so far).
- **Goal**: Accelerate the Ashening. Distribute residuum freely. Help mortals transform. They believe the gods are gone and mortals must become their replacements — and the Ashening is the path to apotheosis.
- **Resources**: Potent residuum stockpiles (harvested from the Shatterlands), powerful Ashen mages, a growing number of converts among the desperate and the hopeful.
- **Limitation**: They're losing members to full Hollowing — the line between "ascended" and "mindless monster" is thin and getting thinner. They can't control the process. Every "ascension" risks creating a dangerous Hollow instead of a new god. The public fears and distrusts them.
- **NPC leader**: **Brother Ashen Kael** — a former priest of the Pale Theocracy who came to the Ember Coast after losing his faith. He's partially Ashened — his left arm is crystalline, his eyes glow faintly blue. He's charismatic, compassionate, and genuinely believes he's offering people a better future. He may be wrong.

**3. The Order of the Pale Flame** (Absolutist / Conservative)
- **Who**: A militant order dedicated to destroying the residuum. They believe the divine blood is a pollutant — a corruption of the natural world left by the gods' violence. They want to burn it all, seal the mines, and let the world find a way forward without it. They see the Ashening as proof that residuum is toxic and the Ashborn as dangerous heretics.
- **Goal**: Destroy all residuum. Seal the Godvein. Shut down the refineries. Force the world to live without divine blood.
- **Resources**: Military training, zealotry, support from rural communities who suffer from residuum-related problems, a network of safe houses, and a surprising amount of old-world knowledge about how to seal divine energy.
- **Limitation**: Destroying residuum means destroying the economy, the magic, and the healing that most people rely on. They're proposing a solution that would cause immediate suffering for millions. They're also small — they can raid and sabotage but can't hold territory.
- **NPC leader**: **Commander Varek Pale** — a former soldier of the Iron Concord who came south after witnessing the Ashening devastate his home town. His wife and daughter became Hollows. He's not crazy — he's grieving, angry, and has a legitimate point about the dangers of residuum. He's also willing to kill for his cause.

**4. The Cartographers' Guild** (Axis Faction)
- **Who**: An organisation of scholars, explorers, and adventurers who map the Shatterlands, study the old world's ruins, and search for knowledge about the Godswar. They're the most neutral faction — they work with everyone because everyone needs their maps and discoveries. They have no stake in who controls the residuum; they just want to understand what happened and what might come next.
- **Goal**: Explore, document, and understand. Find out what the gods were, why they went to war, whether there's a way to fix what's broken (the afterlife, the planar boundaries), and whether there's an alternative to residuum.
- **Resources**: Knowledge, maps, connections to every other faction, a network of frontier outposts, and the loyalty of most adventurers in the region.
- **Limitation**: They're not a military or political power. They can't enforce anything. Their neutrality makes them trusted but also means they have no leverage when factions disagree. And some of their discoveries are dangerous — knowledge can be weaponised.
- **NPC leader**: **Master Cartographer Inera Thale** — a woman in her forties who has explored more of the Shatterlands than anyone alive. She's missing three fingers and an eye to the job. She's calm, curious, and quietly haunted by something she found deep in the Drowned Temple that she hasn't told anyone about yet.

**5. The Resonant** (Wildcard)
- **Who**: People who can hear something in the residuum — a voice, a song, a pulse. Not the gods (they're dead) but... something. The residuum isn't just liquid power — it's the essence of beings who had minds, wills, and memories. The Resonant believe the dead gods are still, in some fragmentary way, conscious. Not alive — but not entirely gone. They want to find a way to communicate with whatever is left of the gods.
- **Goal**: Make contact with the divine remnants. Find out if the gods can be... not revived, but perhaps spoken with. Perhaps they know how to fix what they broke. Perhaps they're sorry. Perhaps they're dreaming and could be awakened — which could be salvation or catastrophe.
- **Resources**: Unique sensitivity to residuum (they can sense deposits, detect Ashening before it manifests, and "hear" divine resonance), a small but growing number of adherents, and the attention of every other faction (some want to use them, some want to silence them).
- **Limitation**: They might be wrong. The "voices" might be echoes, not consciousness — or they might be something else entirely, something from the Far Realm pressing through the weakened planar boundaries. They're a small, new movement, and their claims are unproven and potentially dangerous.
- **NPC leader**: **Sela Resonant** — a young woman, barely twenty, who first heard the voice when she was fourteen, working in the Godvein. She's not a leader by training — she's a miner's daughter who heard something no one else could hear and slowly gathered others who heard it too. She's terrified, certain she's right, and completely unprepared for the political storm she's walking into.

## 6. The Pantheon — The Dead Gods

The gods are dead. Their names are lost. But their influence persists in fragmentary form.

### What Is Known

- There were **many** gods — not a small pantheon of seven or twelve, but a sprawling family of dozens, possibly hundreds, each with their own domain, followers, and agenda.
- They were not aligned along a simple good/evil axis. The Godswar was not Good vs Evil. It was a complex political collapse — alliances, betrayals, competing visions for the world. Every god had a reason to fight, and every reason was, from that god's perspective, justified.
- The gods' **domains** are partially known from the ruins of their temples. Scholars have identified at least:
  - A god of **the forge and making** (whose temples contain the most intact old-world technology)
  - A god of **death and the passage of souls** (whose temples are now the most dangerous — the afterlife break is centered there)
  - A goddess of **growth and the living earth** (whose domains are now the most fertile regions, saturated with residuum)
  - A god of **knowledge and memory** (whose great library was destroyed, but fragments survive)
  - A goddess of **war and protection** (whose orders became the basis for several modern military organisations)
  - A god of **trickery and change** (whose followers claim he saw the Godswar coming and tried to stop it — and failed)
  - A god of **the sea and the depths** (whose temples are underwater and largely unexplored)
  - A goddess of **the stars and fate** (whose followers were the first to predict the Godswar, and were ignored)

- The gods left behind **aspects** — not avatars or manifestations, but persistent magical effects tied to their domains. The Forge God's aspect still makes metal easier to work in certain areas. The Earth Goddess's aspect makes crops grow faster in residuum-rich soil. These are fading.

### Why the Gods' Names Are Lost

When a god dies, their name loses power. The Godswar didn't just kill the gods' bodies — it killed the **concept** of them. People who tried to write down the gods' names after the Shattering found the words wouldn't hold. Ink ran. Stone cracked. Memory slipped. It wasn't a curse — it was a consequence. The gods were so tied to the fabric of reality that their deaths unravelled the parts of reality that held their identity.

This means:
- No one can say the gods' names because no one knows them
- Temple inscriptions are fragmentary and contradictory
- The Resonant hearing "voices" in the residuum may be hearing the gods' lost names trying to re-form
- If a god's name could be recovered, it would be immensely powerful — a direct connection to a dead god's essence

### Divine Magic in Aethermere

- **Clerics**: Draw on ambient divine energy in the earth (residuum-saturated soil). This is weaker than what the gods granted directly. Clerical magic is slowly fading as residuum depletes. Clerics don't worship specific gods (they can't name them) — they venerate "the Divine" as a general concept, or they follow one of the philosophical paths (the Compact's "divine order through structure," the Ashborn's "divine ascension through transformation," the Pale Flame's "divine will through purification").
- **Druids**: Draw on the living earth's energy, which is residuum-suffused. They're more stable than clerics because nature has partially adapted to the residuum. Druids are often the spiritual leaders of rural communities.
- **Paladins**: Their power comes from conviction, not divine granting. A paladin's oath is a self-sustaining magical construct — the intensity of their belief creates a feedback loop that generates divine-like energy. This means a paladin's power doesn't fade with residuum depletion — but it does falter if their faith wavers.
- **Warlocks**: Make pacts with the remnants on the Outer Planes — leaderless celestial and fiendish armies, or whatever else is out there. These pacts are risky — the patrons are not gods, and their motives are unpredictable.
- **Wizards/Sorcerers**: Tap into residuum directly. Wizards do it through study and technique. Sorcerers do it instinctively, often because they have residuum in their blood (a Shattering legacy). Both are dependent on residuum availability — in areas where it's depleted, magic is weaker.

## 7. Character Creation Notes for Aethermere

### Species (Races)

All standard 2024 PHB species are available with Aethermere-specific flavour:
- **Humans**: The majority. Adaptable, ambitious, and the primary drivers of the residuum economy. Most city-dwellers are human.
- **Dwarves**: Master miners and refiners. Dwarven communities are often built around residuum deposits. They have the deepest institutional knowledge of the old world's forge technology.
- **Elves**: Long-lived enough to remember fragments of the Age of Gods. Many elves are melancholic — they've lost more than any other people. The Feywild connection is strained but intact; some elves retreated there and have only recently returned.
- **Halflings**: Rural communities, often in the Saltmarch or similar regions. Strong oral traditions, practical wisdom, and a cultural resistance to residuum dependency.
- **Gnomes**: Tinkers and scholars. Many work with the Cartographers' Guild, studying old-world technology. Gnome artificers are at the forefront of trying to find non-residuum power sources.
- **Dragonborn**: Rare in the Ember Coast. Believed to be descended from the dragons who served the gods during the Godswar. Some communities exist in the Wilds.
- **Tieflings**: People whose ancestors were exposed to high concentrations of residuum during the Shattering. The infernal appearance is a permanent mark of divine blood corruption. They face prejudice but are often naturally gifted with magic.
- **Aasimar**: The mirror of tieflings — ancestors exposed to purer, more celestial divine blood. Rare, often associated with the Pale Theocracy or the Resonant. Their angelic features are fading with each generation.
- **Goliaths**: Mountain dwellers in the Pale Reach. They've adapted to the ash-saturated environment and are resistant to the Ashening. Often work as guards and miners in high-residuum areas.
- **Orcs**: Tribal communities in the Wilds and the fringes of the Shatterlands. They have a strong shamanic tradition and a complicated relationship with residuum — they see it as the blood of their ancestors' gods and treat it with reverence rather than commerce.

### Classes

All 12 core classes work in Aethermere with the following flavour notes:
- **Barbarian**: Primal rage draws on the raw residuum in the blood. Many barbarians come from the Wilds or the Shatterlands.
- **Bard**: Music and story can resonate with the divine remnants in residuum. Bards are often the keepers of oral history in communities that lost their written records.
- **Cleric**: Divine magic drawn from ambient residuum. Weaker than old-world clerics, but genuine. Each cleric follows a philosophical path rather than a specific god.
- **Druid**: Nature magic suffused with residuum. Druids are the spiritual leaders of rural communities and the guardians of the land's fragile balance.
- **Fighter**: No magical dependency. Fighters are the backbone of every faction's military. Weapon Mastery (2024 rules) is especially valued.
- **Monk**: Ki is a form of internalised residuum energy. Monastic orders in the Pale Reach have developed techniques to slow the Ashening through meditation.
- **Paladin**: Power from conviction, not gods. The oath is self-sustaining. Paladins are rare and powerful — their faith is their own.
- **Ranger**: Wilderness experts who navigate the Shatterlands. Most Cartographers' Guild members are rangers or have ranger training.
- **Rogue**: Every city has its underworld. Hallow's Reach's thieves' guild is particularly active, stealing refined residuum.
- **Sorcerer**: Born with residuum in the blood. Often viewed with suspicion — they're living proof of the Shattering's lasting effects.
- **Warlock**: Pacts with Outer Planes remnants. Risky, powerful, and morally complex. What do the leaderless celestials and fiends want?
- **Wizard**: Scholars of residuum-based magic. The most educated spellcasters, but dependent on residuum availability and vulnerable to the Ashening from prolonged magical work.

### Backgrounds

Consider these Aethermere-specific backgrounds (in addition to standard ones):
- **Residuum Miner**: You worked in the Godvein or another deposit. You know the mines, the risks, and the smell of raw divine blood.
- **Shatterlands Scout**: You've been beyond the safe regions. You've seen what the Godswar did to the land.
- **Refinery Worker**: You processed raw residuum into refined forms. You know the industry and its secrets.
- **Temple Orphan**: Raised in the ruins of an Age of Gods temple. You know fragments of old rituals and prayers.
- **Ashened (Partial)**: You've been exposed to high residuum concentrations and show early signs of the Ashening — a faint grey pallor, slightly luminescent eyes. You're not Hollow, but you know what you might become.
- **Cartographer's Apprentice**: Trained by the Guild in mapping, exploration, and old-world lore.

## 8. Campaign Arc — Broad Strokes

### Design Principle

Using the tier-based pacing from our research: Tier 1 (levels 1-4) is local, Tier 2 (5-10) is regional, Tier 3 (11-15) is world-shaping.

### Act 1: The Ember Coast (Levels 1-4)

**The Setup**: The player characters arrive in Hallow's Reach as newcomers — adventurers looking for work, drawn by the promise of the Shatterlands. They start as small-time: guarding caravans, exploring minor ruins, dealing with local problems. They meet the factions, take jobs, and begin to understand the world's situation.

**Key Events**:
- Discover that the Godvein is running low — Lady Hallow is keeping it secret
- Encounter their first Hollow in the Shatterlands — learn what the Ashening does
- Meet Sela Resonant and hear her claims about the voices
- Get drawn into a faction conflict — two factions want the same Shatterlands salvage site
- Discover the Drowned Temple and find a fragment of an old-world text that hints at a deeper truth about the Godswar

**End of Act 1**: The characters have chosen (or been forced into) a faction alignment, understand the central conflict, and have a personal stake in the outcome. They're ~level 5 and ready for bigger things.

### Act 2: The Wider Conflict (Levels 5-10)

**The Escalation**: The residuum shortage becomes public. Panic spreads. Faction conflict intensifies. The characters are now significant players — they have reputation, connections, and power.

**Key Events**:
- The Iron Concord sends envoys (or an army) to the Ember Coast, seeking to control the Godvein
- The Ashborn make a major move — a ritual to mass-Ashen a community, or an attempt to crack open a sealed deep deposit
- The Pale Flame launches a major sabotage attack on the refinery
- The Resonant make contact with... something. It might be a dead god's fragment. It might be something else.
- The characters discover that the Godswar wasn't what everyone thinks — the gods didn't just fight each other. Something else was involved. Something from outside.
- A planar incursion — the weakened boundaries allow something from the Outer Planes or the Far Realm to break through

**End of Act 2**: The situation is at crisis point. The characters are ~level 11 and must make a choice that will shape the world's future.

### Act 3: What Comes After (Levels 11-15+)

**The Resolution**: The characters must decide what to do with the knowledge and power they've accumulated. Can the afterlife be fixed? Can the planar boundaries be restored? Should residuum be preserved, destroyed, distributed, or used for something unprecedented? The final confrontation depends on the characters' choices and faction alignments.

**Possible Endings** (depending on player choices):
- **Restoration**: Find a way to restart the cosmic machinery without the gods — not bringing them back, but building something new. The afterlife works again, planar boundaries stabilise, but magic changes fundamentally.
- **Ascension**: The Ashborn are right — mortals absorb the remaining residuum and become the new gods. The world transforms. Some people become something greater; many others become Hollows. Was it worth it?
- **Purification**: The Pale Flame's vision — destroy the residuum, accept the loss of magic, and rebuild the world without divine power. Hard, painful, and possibly the most honest path.
- **Catastrophe**: The wrong thing comes through the broken planar boundaries. The characters must stop it, and the cost is everything. A pyrrhic victory — the world survives but is changed beyond recognition.
