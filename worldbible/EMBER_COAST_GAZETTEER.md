# Ember Coast Gazetteer

> Companion to the Ember Coast region map. Detailed location reference for DM use.

---

## Region Overview

The Ember Coast is a coastal region on the eastern edge of the known world. It was the site of a major battle during the Godswar — the gods' blood soaked deep into the earth here, making it one of the richest residuum regions in existence. The region spans approximately 80 miles north-to-south and 60 miles east-to-west, bounded by the Wide Sea to the east, the Shatterlands to the west, the Pale Reach mountains to the north, and the Saltmarch wetlands to the south.

### Climate

Temperate maritime. Winters are cold and wet (the divine ash in the northern mountains creates a permanent chill wind from the Pale Reach). Summers are mild with frequent fog along the coast — the "ember fog," faintly luminescent from residuum particles in the air. The fog is harmless but unnerving to newcomers.

## Major Settlements

### Hallow's Reach (Population: ~40,000)

The largest city in the Ember Coast and the seat of the Hallow Compact. Built on the ruins of a holy city from the Age of Gods — the old temple stones are visible in the city's oldest walls, their inscriptions worn smooth.

**Layout:**
- **The Upper City**: On a hill overlooking the harbour. The Hallow family mansion, the administrative buildings, the officers of the City Guard, and the wealthy merchant homes. Walled. Clean. Well-lit with residuum lamps.
- **The Lower City**: Spreads from the hill down to the harbour. Dense, crowded, noisy. Workshops, residuum refineries (the smoke from these gives the city its permanent haze), taverns, market squares, and the homes of the working class.
- **The Harbour**: The economic heart. Ships from the Silver Isles and other coastal cities dock here, trading raw residuum for refined product, food, and goods. The harbour is fortified — the Iron Concord has been making noises about "securing" the residuum supply.
- **The Godvein Entrance**: North of the city, a massive stone doorway carved into a hillside. This is the entrance to the richest residuum mine in the region. Heavily guarded. Workers enter at dawn, leave at dusk. The tunnel system extends miles underground.

**Key Locations Within the City:**
- **The Refinery Quarter**: Where raw residuum is processed. Steam, glow, and the constant hum of machinery. Workers here have the highest Ashening rates.
- **The Copper Square**: The main market. You can buy anything here — including things the Hallow Compact would rather you didn't.
- **The Dead Temple**: An Age of Gods temple in the Lower City, half-collapsed. Now used as a shelter for the homeless and a meeting place for the Resonant (they say the acoustics carry the voices better here).
- **The Hallow Manor**: The seat of government. Part fortress, part mansion. Lady Seren Hallow holds court here.
- **The Cartographers' Hall**: The Ember Coast headquarters of the Cartographers' Guild. A stone building filled with maps, charts, and expedition records. Open to all — neutrality is strictly enforced.

**Atmosphere:** Wealthy but tense. The streets are well-patrolled, but there's an undercurrent of anxiety — people have heard rumours about the Godvein running low. Refined residuum prices have been creeping up. The Ashborn have been preaching in the poorer quarters. Pale Flame agents are suspected of sabotage at Refinery #3 last month.

### Emberwood Village (Population: ~800)

A frontier town five miles from the Shatterlands' eastern edge. The last stop before the dangerous territory. Where adventurers stock up, rest, and plan expeditions.

**Layout:**
- A single main street with taverns, supply stores, and a small shrine.
- Houses spread out in a loose cluster, surrounded by cleared forest (the clearing is maintained for defense — things come out of the Shatterlands at night).
- A wooden palisade, more symbolic than practical — it won't stop anything truly dangerous.

**Key Locations:**
- **The Black Root Tavern**: The main gathering place. Run by **Armin Gainsbury**, a retired adventurer who lost his left hand to a Hollow. He knows the Shatterlands as well as anyone and sells information along with ale. The tavern is the unofficial hiring hall for adventuring parties.
- **Gainsbury's Supply Store**: Next to the tavern. Sells ropes, torches, residuum lamps, climbing gear, rations, and the occasional magic item scavenged from the Shatterlands. Prices are 30% higher than in Hallow's Reach.
- **The Shrine of Small Things**: A modest shrine maintained by a druid named **Henna Moss**. Not dedicated to any god (they're dead) but to "the small good things that survive." Henna offers healing, herbal remedies, and practical advice about surviving the Shatterlands. She can sense the Ashening in its early stages — a useful service for adventurers who work with residuum.
- **The Cartographers' Outpost**: A small stone tower on the village's western edge. Staffed by two Guild cartographers who maintain maps of the Shatterlands and record expedition reports. They can hire parties for survey work.

**Atmosphere:** Rough, practical, surprisingly friendly. Everyone here is either an adventurer, supports adventurers, or used to be an adventurer. There's a camaraderie born of shared danger. The mood shifts when a party comes back with losses — the whole village feels it.

## The Shatterlands

The scarred region west of Emberwood Village. The Godswar's final battle happened here — the land was literally shattered. The terrain is unstable, dangerous, and rich in residuum.

### Terrain Features

- **Gravity Anomalies**: In patches, gravity is weaker or stronger than normal. Objects float. People are pinned. The patches shift slowly — a safe path one week may be dangerous the next.
- **Elemental Seeps**: Where the planar boundaries are weakest, elemental energy leaks through. Patches of fire, pools of acid, sudden ice formations, or air that becomes solid. Unpredictable.
- **Residuum Pools**: Luminous lakes of raw divine blood. The richest deposits are here — but the concentration is high enough that unprotected exposure causes Ashening within hours.
- **Ruins**: Age of Gods structures, broken and half-buried. Some are intact enough to explore. Many contain old-world artifacts, documents, or guardians (automated defenses that still function after 300 years).
- **Cracks**: Deep fissures in the earth, some bottomless, some leading to underground cavern systems. The Godvein extends into this network — the deep tunnels connect to the Shatterlands underground.

### Known Adventure Sites in the Shatterlands

**1. The Drowned Temple**
- Half-submerged in a residuum lake at the centre of the Shatterlands
- An Age of Gods temple dedicated to a forgotten god — possibly the god of death or souls (the imagery suggests passage to the afterlife)
- Contains intact murals, a central altar, and a sealed vault beneath the altar that has never been opened
- The residuum lake around it is concentrated enough to cause Ashening within 2-3 hours of exposure
- Previous expeditions have retrieved fragments of old-world text from the outer chambers
- **Master Cartographer Inera Thale** found something here she won't talk about
- Recommended level: 3-5 for the outer chambers, 7+ for the sealed vault

**2. The Broken Spire**
- A tower that was sheared in half during the Godswar. The top half is missing. The bottom half leans at a 30-degree angle but is still standing.
- Five floors of rooms, partially collapsed. Contains a working old-world mechanism — some kind of communication or signalling device.
- Previously inhabited by a Hollow that has since moved on. May contain its "nest" — residuum crystals and personal effects from its former life.
- Recommended level: 2-4

**3. The Ash Fields**
- An open area where the ground is covered in a fine, luminous ash — concentrated residuum dust
- Extremely dangerous — breathing the ash accelerates Ashening dramatically
- Rich in surface-deposited residuum crystals (can be harvested with proper equipment)
- Home to **Ash Wraiths** — semi-corporeal beings that may be the remnants of people who fully Ashened here long ago
- The Ashborn make pilgrimages here, claiming the ash "sings" to them
- Recommended level: 4-6

**4. The Undermarch**
- An underground tunnel system connecting the Godvein to the Shatterlands' deep caverns
- Partly mapped by the Cartographers' Guild, partly unexplored
- Contains underground rivers of liquid residuum (the purest form)
- Inhabited by subterranean creatures adapted to residuum exposure — blind, luminescent, and territorial
- The deeper tunnels show signs of Age of Gods construction — carved walls, old tracks, maintenance shafts
- Recommended level: 5-8

**5. The War Memorial**
- A massive stone statue in the Shatterlands, half-buried. It depicts two figures — possibly gods — locked in combat. One figure's head is missing. The other's face is strangely serene.
- The statue radiates a powerful magical aura. Spellcasters near it feel their magic surge — spells cast within 30 feet are empowered but unpredictable.
- Scholars debate whether the statue is a memorial built by survivors or a petrified god (the Astral Plane has petrified god corpses — could one have fallen here?)
- No one has been able to damage or move the statue
- Recommended level: 3-5 (exterior), 8+ (if the statue is actually a petrified god and something wakes it)

## The Pale Reach (Northern Mountains)

Ash-covered mountains north of the Ember Coast. The divine ash from the Godswar never settled here — the mountains are perpetually grey-white. Cold, sparse, and oddly peaceful.

### Key Locations

**Greyveil Monastery**
- A monastic order perched on a mountainside, accessible by a three-day climb from the lowlands
- The monks have developed meditation techniques that slow the Ashening — drawing internal residuum energy into stable patterns rather than letting it corrupt the body
- They offer training to anyone willing to make the climb, regardless of faction affiliation
- The monastery also maintains a "dissolution chamber" — a ritual space where they help restless souls dissolve peacefully into ambient energy (their solution to the broken afterlife)
- The monks know more about the old gods than most — their oral traditions predate the Godswar
- Led by **Abbot Theren**, a sixty-year-old human monk whose eyes have the faint blue luminescence of early Ashening. He's been slowing it for thirty years.

**The Ash Pass**
- A mountain pass leading north toward the Pale Theocracy
- Currently closed — the Theocracy has sealed their border, citing "spiritual quarantine"
- The pass itself is treacherous — ash storms, low visibility, and occasional elemental anomalies
- Adventurers sometimes try to cross anyway. Most come back. Some don't.

## The Saltmarch (Southern Wetlands)

Coastal wetlands south of Hallow's Reach. Poor in residuum. The people here live simply and have a strong oral tradition.

### Key Locations

**Reedwatch (Population: ~300)**
- The largest Saltmarch village. Built on stilts over the marsh water.
- Known for their **ancestor binding** tradition — they ritually bind the souls of their dead as protective spirits. The spirits guard the village, guide the living, and provide continuity with the past.
- The village elder, **Mara Reedwatch**, is a druid who maintains the binding rituals. She's ninety years old and sharp as a blade.
- Reedwatch is suspicious of "blood magic" (residuum-based magic) but not hostile. They'll trade fish, reed crafts, and information for goods they can't make themselves.
- Their ancestor spirits can sense the Ashening — they become agitated near people with high residuum exposure

**Mire's End (Population: ~150)**
- A smaller, poorer village at the southern edge of the Saltmarch
- The people here are fishermen and reed-gatherers
- They've been experiencing problems — something in the marsh has been killing livestock at night. Tracks suggest something large, possibly a mutated creature from the Shatterlands that wandered south
- This is a good low-level adventure hook for a starting party

## The Godvein

The richest residuum deposit in the Ember Coast, and the economic foundation of Hallow's Reach.

### Description

A vast underground network of tunnels and caverns where divine blood pools in luminous lakes. The entrance is a massive stone doorway north of Hallow's Reach, carved into a hillside during the Age of Gods (the doorway was already there — the Hallow family didn't build it, they found it).

### Structure

- **Upper Tunnels (Levels 1-3)**: Mapped, secured, and actively mined. Residuum concentration is moderate. Workers do 8-hour shifts with mandatory rest periods. Ashening risk is low with proper precautions (residuum masks, rotation).
- **Middle Tunnels (Levels 4-7)**: Partially mapped. Higher concentration. Only experienced miners work here. Mandatory weekly Ashening screenings. Several sections are sealed off due to cave-ins or "incidents" (the Compact doesn't discuss these publicly).
- **Deep Caverns (Levels 8+)**: Largely unexplored. The residuum here is nearly pure — liquid light in vast underground lakes. The Ashening risk is extreme — minutes of unprotected exposure can begin the transformation. Strange sounds echo from below. Some miners report hearing voices.
- **Connection to the Undermarch**: The deepest tunnels connect to the Undermarch tunnel system under the Shatterlands. The Compact has sealed this connection, but the seals are deteriorating.

### Current Status

The upper tunnels are showing clear signs of depletion. Output has dropped 15% in the last year. Lady Hallow has kept this secret, but the refinery workers are starting to notice the reduced raw material. The middle tunnels are being pushed harder than safety guidelines recommend. There's pressure to open the deep caverns for full-scale mining, but the Ashening risk makes this effectively a death sentence for the miners.

This is the ticking clock of the campaign: the residuum is running out, and no one has a solution that doesn't involve either catastrophic risk or fundamental change.

## Travel and Navigation

### Roads

- **The Old Trade Road**: Runs east-west from the distant city of Altberg (off-map) to Hallow's Reach. Three weeks' travel on foot. Caravans use this route to bring supplies to the Ember Coast. Moderately safe — bandits are the main threat, not monsters.
- **The Emberwood Road**: Runs from Hallow's Reach to Emberwood Village. Two days on foot, one on horseback. Patrolled irregularly by the Compact's guards.
- **The Salt Coast Road**: Runs south from Hallow's Reach along the coast to the Saltmarch villages. Three days on foot. Safe but lonely.
- **The Mountain Trail**: Unmarked path from the lowlands to Greyveil Monastery. Four days, steep and cold. No bandits — the monks' reputation keeps people from causing trouble on the approach.

### Off-Road Travel

- **Through the Shatterlands**: No roads. Navigation is difficult — landmarks shift, gravity anomalies make compasses unreliable. The Cartographers' Guild sells updated maps (50 gold for a current Shatterlands map, 200 gold for a detailed survey of a specific area). Survival checks required. Random encounters: Ash Wraiths, Hollows, elemental anomalies, unstable terrain.
- **Through the Pale Reach**: Mountain trails, cold and ash-covered. Survival checks for cold weather. The monks maintain the trail to Greyveil, but beyond that, it's wilderness.
- **Through the Saltmarch**: Wetland travel — knee-deep water, treacherous ground, mosquitoes. No major dangers but slow going (half normal travel speed).

### Sea Travel

- Ships from the Silver Isles dock at Hallow's Reach every 2-3 weeks, weather permitting
- Coastal fishing boats operate from the Saltmarch
- The Wide Sea is largely unexplored beyond the known trade routes — no one knows what lies to the east
