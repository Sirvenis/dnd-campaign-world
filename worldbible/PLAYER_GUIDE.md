# Aethermere — Player's Guide

> A spoiler-free introduction to the world for players. Read this before character creation.

---

## The World You're Entering

Three centuries ago, the gods went to war and killed each other. Every single one. The sky cracked, the earth shook, and their divine blood soaked into the ground. The world broke — the afterlife stopped working, magic went haywire, and civilization collapsed.

It's been 300 years. People rebuilt. The divine blood in the earth — called **residuum** — turned out to be useful. It powers magic, fuels machines, heals the sick, and lights the cities. It's the most valuable substance in the world.

But it's running out. And it's dangerous.

---

## The Key Facts

**The gods are dead.** Not missing, not withdrawn, not sleeping. Dead. Their bodies are stone mountains drifting in the Astral Plane. No one knows their names — when a god dies, their name unravels from reality itself. You can't write it. You can't remember it. The temples have blank spaces where the names should be.

**The afterlife is broken.** When people die, their souls have nowhere to go. Some linger as ghosts. Some dissolve into ambient energy. Some become something else. Cultures have developed different ways to deal with this — some bind souls as ancestor spirits, some help them dissolve peacefully, some just try not to think about it.

**Residuum is divine blood.** It's a luminescent liquid that seeps from the earth. It glows white-gold, blue-violet, or sickly green. It's beautiful. It's also the remains of dead gods, and spending too much time around it changes you.

**The Ashening.** Prolonged exposure to residuum causes a physical and mental transformation. Your skin goes grey. Your eyes start to glow. Eventually, you lose yourself — becoming a **Hollow**: powerful, mindless, and dangerous. This takes years of heavy exposure. Casual contact with diluted residuum (what's in the air, in lamps, in most magic) is safe. But miners, refiners, and adventurers who work with concentrated residuum are at risk.

**Magic works, but differently.** There's no Weave anymore (the gods maintained that). Spellcasters tap into residuum in the earth instead. Clerics draw on ambient divine energy — it's real, but it's fading, and no god is granting it. Paladins power themselves through sheer conviction. Warlocks make pacts with the remnants of celestial and fiendish armies on the Outer Planes — armies that have no commanders anymore. Wizards and sorcerers tap residuum directly.

## The Ember Coast

You're starting in the **Ember Coast**, a region on the eastern edge of the known world. It's one of the richest residuum areas because a major battle of the Godswar happened here — the gods' blood soaked deep into the earth.

**Hallow's Reach** is the main city. Population 40,000. Built on the ruins of a holy city from the Age of Gods. It controls the **Godvein**, the richest residuum mine in the region. The city is wealthy, crowded, and tense. Refineries line the harbour. The ruling Hallow family controls the mine and the economy.

**The Shatterlands** are to the west. The ground is cracked and unstable. Gravity doesn't always work right. Elemental energy leaks through broken planar boundaries. Ancient ruins lie half-buried. It's rich in residuum and old-world artifacts — and it's dangerous. This is where adventurers go to make their fortunes.

**Emberwood Village** is the last stop before the Shatterlands. A frontier town where adventurers stock up, find work, and drink. You'll probably start here, or pass through it.

## The Five Factions

You'll encounter five major factions in the Ember Coast. Each has a different vision for the future. None of them is entirely right or entirely wrong. You can work with any of them, oppose any of them, or try to play them against each other.

**The Hallow Compact** — The city's ruling powers. They control the Godvein and the refineries. They want to keep things as they are: stable, profitable, and under their control. The residuum is running out, but they'd rather keep that quiet than admit the system is failing. Led by Lady Seren Hallow, a pragmatic matriarch in her sixties.

**The Ashborn** — People who have undergone partial Ashening and embraced it. They believe the residuum is a gift — that mortals should absorb it and become the new gods. The Ashening, they say, is not corruption but transformation. The problem: the line between "ascending" and "becoming a mindless monster" is thin and getting thinner. Led by Brother Ashen Kael, a former priest with a crystalline arm and glowing blue eyes.

**The Order of the Pale Flame** — A militant order that wants to destroy all residuum. They see it as a pollutant — toxic divine blood that corrupts everything it touches. They're not wrong about the danger. But destroying residuum means destroying the magic, healing, and economy that most people rely on. Led by Commander Varek Pale, a grieving soldier whose wife and daughter became Hollows.

**The Cartographers' Guild** — Explorers and scholars who map the Shatterlands and study the old world. They're neutral — they work with everyone because everyone needs their maps. They have no political agenda; they just want to understand what happened and what might come next. Led by Master Cartographer Inera Thale, who has explored more of the Shatterlands than anyone alive and is haunted by something she found there.

**The Resonant** — A new, small movement of people who hear something in the residuum. A voice. A song. Not the gods (they're dead) but... something. Fragments of consciousness, maybe. The residuum is the essence of beings who had minds — perhaps something of those minds persists. The Resonant want to make contact. This could be salvation — or it could be something far worse. Led by Sela, a young miner's daughter who first heard the voice at fourteen.

## What Your Character Knows

- You know the gods are dead. Everyone knows this. It's not a secret or a mystery — it's the foundational fact of the world.
- You know about residuum. You've seen it in lamps, in magic items, in the air on foggy days. You know it's valuable and you know it's dangerous in high concentrations.
- You know about the Ashening. You've seen Hollows, or at least heard stories. They're tragic and terrifying.
- You know the five factions exist, at least by reputation. You may have sympathies toward one or another.
- You know the Shatterlands are dangerous but rich. Adventurers go there and come back with wealth — or don't come back.
- You know the afterlife is broken. You've probably lost someone whose soul didn't pass on. This is a universal experience in Aethermere.

## What Your Character Doesn't Know

- The Godvein is running low. The Hallow Compact is keeping this secret.
- What Master Cartographer Inera Thale found in the Drowned Temple.
- What the voices the Resonant hear actually are.
- Why the gods went to war. The true cause is lost to history — and may not be what anyone thinks.
- What's pressing at the edges of reality through the weakened planar boundaries.

## Character Creation Quick Guide

### Species
All standard D&D 2024 species are available. The most common in the Ember Coast are humans, dwarves (miners and refiners), and halflings (rural communities). Tieflings (ancestors exposed to residuum during the Shattering) and aasimar (ancestors exposed to purer celestial blood) exist but are uncommon. Dragonborn are rare. Goliaths are found in the northern mountains.

### Classes
All 12 core classes work. The flavour changes to fit the world:
- **Clerics** draw on ambient divine energy in the earth — real but fading, and not granted by any god
- **Paladins** are powered by their own conviction, not divine granting
- **Warlocks** pact with leaderless celestial/fiendish remnants on the Outer Planes
- **Sorcerers** may have residuum in their blood (a Shattering legacy)
- **Wizards** tap residuum through study and technique
- **Druids** draw on the residuum-suffused living earth
- **Monks** internalise residuum energy as ki

### Backgrounds (Aethermere-specific)
In addition to standard backgrounds, consider:
- **Residuum Miner** — worked in the Godvein or another mine
- **Shatterlands Scout** — been beyond the safe regions
- **Refinery Worker** — processed raw residuum
- **Temple Orphan** — raised in ruins of an Age of Gods temple
- **Ashened (Partial)** — early signs of the Ashening, faint grey pallor, slightly luminescent eyes
- **Cartographer's Apprentice** — trained by the Guild in mapping and exploration

### Important Questions for Your Character
1. How do you feel about residuum? Is it a gift, a curse, or just a fact of life?
2. Have you lost anyone to the Ashening? To the broken afterlife?
3. Which faction (if any) appeals to you, and why?
4. Why are you coming to the Ember Coast? What are you looking for?
5. What would you die for? What would you *not* die for?
